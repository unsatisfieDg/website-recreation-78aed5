CERTIFICATES OF ANALYSIS / A READING GUIDE

THE DETAILS.
In plain sight.

A Certificate of Analysis records laboratory findings for a tested sample. Here is where to start when reading one.

Look closer.
Read the original.

Visit the main EHF website for its existing certificates. Always check the product, batch and test date.

Visit EHF’s certificates
01 / IDENTIFICATION

Does the report match?

Start with the product name and batch or sample identifier. A report for a different batch is not the same as a report for the product in your hands.

  • Product or sample name
  • Batch, lot or sample identifier
  • Laboratory name and report date
02 / CANNABINOID PROFILE

Read the numbers with their units.

Look at which compounds were tested and how their amounts are expressed. Percentages, milligrams per gram and milligrams per serving are different measurements. Read the report’s notes for its definitions and reporting limits.

03 / TEST SCOPE

What was actually tested?

Some reports cover cannabinoid content. Others include additional panels such as pesticides, heavy metals, residual solvents or microbial testing. Check the scope on the original report rather than assuming every panel is included.

04 / FOLLOW-UP

Keep asking good questions.

If a product’s report is missing, unclear or does not match its batch, ask the EHF team for the relevant document and explanation.

Contact the EHF team

This page is a reading guide, not a laboratory certificate. Verified EU product and batch documents will need to be supplied before they can be displayed here.
